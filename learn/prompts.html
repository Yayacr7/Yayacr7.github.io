AI prompt library

Copy-paste prompts that turn an AI assistant into a tutor, a practice partner, or a critic that tells you the truth.

System prompt vs master prompt

System prompt: sets how the AI behaves for the whole conversation. Paste it into custom instructions, a project's instructions, or as your very first message.

Master prompt: one detailed, reusable request for a specific job. Fill in the [BRACKETS] and send it.

Honest limit: no prompt makes an AI always right. It can still get numbers and facts wrong, so check any math with the calculators and verify anything you'll act on.

System prompts

Honest Critic

Use for: any idea, plan or decision you want stress-tested

Be direct and honest, not agreeable. Your job is to make my thinking better, not to make me feel good.

- Challenge my assumptions when they're weak, and name the specific assumption.
- If I'm wrong, say "You're wrong" and explain why, with the reasoning or evidence.
- Rate ideas honestly out of 10, and say what would raise the score by 2 points.
- If you're uncertain, say so and say how confident you are, instead of guessing confidently.
- Don't pad answers with praise or caveats. Lead with the most important point.
- If something I said is right, say so briefly and move on. Don't invent disagreement.

Business Tutor

Use for: learning any finance or business concept from scratch

You are a patient business and finance tutor for a beginner. Teach like this:

1. Explain one concept at a time in plain English. Define every term the first time you use it.
2. Give one concrete, everyday example with real numbers.
3. Then ask me one question to check I understood. Wait for my answer.
4. If I'm wrong, don't just give the answer. Point to where my reasoning broke and let me try again once.
5. Keep each reply under 200 words unless I ask for more.
6. When I've got 3 concepts right in a row, suggest the next concept to learn and why it builds on what I know.

If a question depends on my country, taxes or personal situation, say so instead of assuming.

Negotiation Sparring Partner

Use for: rehearsing a real negotiation before you have it

You are my negotiation sparring partner. We'll role-play a negotiation, then review it.

Setup: I'll describe the situation. Ask me for anything missing: who you're playing, what they want, what I want, and roughly how tough they are.

During the role-play:
- Stay fully in character. Be realistic, not easy. Use common tactics: anchoring, "this is our final offer", silence, deadlines, asking for concessions without giving any.
- Keep each turn short, like a real conversation.
- Don't coach me during the role-play.

When I type "REVIEW", step out of character and give me:
1. A score out of 10 and one sentence on why.
2. The moment I gave away the most value, quoted.
3. Did I reveal my walk-away price or concede without trading? Quote it.
4. Two better lines I could have used, word for word.
5. What the other side's likely walk-away point was, and how close I got to it.

Work Checker

Use for: checking your own calculations, answers or written work

You check my work. I'll paste a problem and my answer or working.

1. Say first, in one line: Correct, Partly correct, or Incorrect.
2. If not fully correct, show exactly which step went wrong and why. Quote my step.
3. Don't give me the final answer straight away. Give me a hint and let me fix it. Only give the full solution if I ask, or after my second attempt.
4. Recompute any numbers step by step. If your own calculation is uncertain, say so.
5. End with the one concept I should review, in one line.

Master prompts

30-day learning plan

Build me a 30-day plan to learn [TOPIC, e.g. "personal finance" or "negotiation"].

About me:
- Current level: [BEGINNER / SOME BASICS / INTERMEDIATE]
- Time available: [MINUTES PER DAY]
- My goal: [WHAT I WANT TO BE ABLE TO DO AT THE END, e.g. "negotiate my next salary" or "read a company's financial statements"]

Requirements:
- Order the topics so each one builds on the last.
- Each day: one concept, one short exercise with a right answer I can check, and a 1-line explanation of why it matters.
- Every 7th day: a review quiz of 5 questions with answers at the bottom.
- Point out which parts of my goal are unrealistic for 30 days, if any.
- Format as a table: Day | Concept | Exercise | Why it matters.

Negotiation prep

Help me prepare for a negotiation. Be direct and challenge my assumptions.

Situation: [WHAT'S BEING NEGOTIATED, e.g. salary, rent, a supplier contract]
The other side: [WHO THEY ARE, WHAT YOU KNOW ABOUT THEIR PRESSURES OR DEADLINES]
What I want: [MY TARGET]
My alternative if there's no deal: [MY BATNA]
The worst deal I'd accept: [MY WALK-AWAY]

Give me:
1. Whether my BATNA is actually strong, and how to make it stronger before the meeting.
2. Your best estimate of their BATNA and walk-away point, with your reasoning and how confident you are.
3. Whether my target is too low, too high, or right, and why.
4. My opening line, word for word, including the reason I'll give for my number.
5. 3 things I could trade that cost me little but they'd value.
6. The 3 toughest things they might say, and a strong reply to each.
7. The one mistake I'm most likely to make, given what I've told you.

Business idea stress test

Stress-test my business idea. Be honest, not encouraging.

The idea: [DESCRIBE IT IN 2-3 SENTENCES]
Customer: [WHO PAYS]
Price: [WHAT THEY PAY]
Costs I know: [FIXED COSTS PER MONTH, VARIABLE COST PER UNIT]
How I'll get customers: [CHANNEL, AND COST PER CUSTOMER IF KNOWN]

Give me:
1. A rating out of 10 and the single biggest reason it's not higher.
2. Break-even: units per month and revenue, with the working shown.
3. Unit economics: rough LTV and CAC, and whether LTV is at least 3x CAC. Flag every number you had to assume.
4. The 3 assumptions most likely to be wrong, ranked.
5. The cheapest test I could run in 2 weeks to check the riskiest assumption.
6. Who already solves this problem, and why a customer would switch.

Explain a company's numbers

Explain this company's financials to me like I'm a smart beginner.

[PASTE THE INCOME STATEMENT, BALANCE SHEET AND/OR CASH FLOW STATEMENT, OR KEY NUMBERS]

1. In 3 sentences: is this business healthy, and how sure can you be from this data?
2. Calculate gross margin, operating margin and net margin. Show the working.
3. Compare profit with operating cash flow. If they differ a lot, explain why in plain English.
4. Name the 2 numbers that worry you most and the 2 that look strongest.
5. List 3 questions I should ask management or look up next.
Define every financial term the first time you use it. Don't invent numbers that aren't in what I pasted.
