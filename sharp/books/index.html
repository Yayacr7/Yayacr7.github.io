Jim Collins · 2001

Good to Great

9/10 Must read

Read it for: Why a few companies go from good to great, and most never do. Based on years of research comparing companies that made the leap with similar ones that didn't.

The main quotes, in plain English

  1. Top quote

    “Good is the enemy of great.”

    Chapter 1

    What it means: "Good enough" feels safe. That comfort is exactly what stops most people and businesses from becoming great.

    Try this: Pick one thing you do "good enough", like your product photos or how fast you reply. Make it great this week.

  2. “…first get the right people on the bus, the wrong people off the bus, and the right people in the right seats…”

    p. 41

    What it means: Who you build with matters more than your first plan. With the right people, you can fix a plan. With the wrong ones, no plan works.

    Try this: Before you pick a partner or first helper, ask: would I still want this person if our idea changed?

  3. “Level 5 leaders are a study in duality: modest and willful, humble and fearless.”

    p. 22–23

    What it means: The best leaders don't show off, and they never give up. Quiet and stubborn beats loud and flashy.

    Try this: When something goes well, credit others. When it goes badly, look at yourself first.

  4. “You can't manufacture passion or 'motivate' people to feel passionate.”

    p. 109

    What it means: You can't fake caring for long. Build around something you, and the people with you, actually care about.

    Try this: List 3 things you'd happily do for free. Does your business idea touch at least one?

  5. “They had freedom, but within a framework.”

    p. 123

    What it means: A few clear rules, plus freedom inside them, beats both bossing everyone around and having no rules at all.

    Try this: Write 3 rules for your business, like "reply to every customer within 24 hours". Decide everything else freely.

The big idea, in our words: The flywheel: great results come from many small pushes in the same direction, not one big lucky moment.

⚠ Be careful: Being great once doesn't last by itself. Circuit City, one of the book's great companies, went out of business in 2009, and Fannie Mae needed a government rescue in 2008. The habits have to continue.

Michael Masterson · 2008

Ready, Fire, Aim

8/10 Read it

Read it for: How to get a business from zero to real sales fast, and how your job as the founder changes as it grows.

The main quotes, in plain English

  1. Top quote

    “Nothing matters more than selling.”

    Stage one

    What it means: At the start, your number one job is getting paying customers. Not the logo, the business cards or the perfect website.

    Try this: This week, spend more time asking people to buy than on design.

  2. “The Ready, Fire, Aim concept is about velocity, about the profound benefits of moving from an idea into action…”

    p. 181

    What it means: Speed wins. Launch a simple version, then improve it using what real customers tell you.

    Try this: Launch a basic version of your idea within 7 days, even if it's small.

  3. “It's Ready, Fire, Aim, not Fire at Will.”

    p. 181

    What it means: Fast doesn't mean careless. Get ready first: know who buys, at what price, and how they'll hear about you. Then launch.

    Try this: Before you launch, write one line each: who buys, the price, and how they'll find you.

The big idea, in our words: A business grows in stages, and the founder's main job changes: first salesperson, then innovator, then manager, then investor. Test an idea cheaply before you put most of your money into it, and decide in advance what result means "keep going".

⚠ Be careful: It's written for building very big businesses; the subtitle is "Zero to $100 Million in No Time Flat". For you, "Fire" should mean a small, cheap test, not spending your savings.

T. Harv Eker · 2005

Secrets of the Millionaire Mind

6/10 Skim it

Read it for: Spotting your money habits and beliefs. The book is built around 17 "wealth files" that compare how rich and poor people think.

The main quotes, in plain English

  1. Top quote

    “Rich people act in spite of fear. Poor people let fear stop them.”

    Wealth file 16

    What it means: Everyone feels scared before their first sales message or price increase. Feeling fear is normal; letting it decide for you is the problem.

    Try this: Send one message you've been putting off today.

  2. “Rich people focus on opportunities. Poor people focus on obstacles.”

    Wealth file 5

    What it means: See the problems, but spend most of your energy on what could work.

    Try this: For every problem you list about your idea, write one opportunity next to it.

  3. “Rich people focus on their net worth. Poor people focus on their working income.”

    Wealth file 13

    What it means: What you own minus what you owe matters more than any one paycheck.

    Try this: Work out your net worth: savings plus things worth money, minus any debts.

  4. “Rich people manage their money well. Poor people mismanage their money well.”

    Wealth file 14

    What it means: A joke with a point: being "good" at spending everything is still a skill, just aimed the wrong way.

    Try this: Track every dollar for one week with the budget planner.

  5. “Rich people constantly learn and grow. Poor people think they already know.”

    Wealth file 17

    What it means: The moment you think you know it all, you stop getting better.

    Try this: Do one Sharp lesson a day for a week.

⚠ Be careful: Splitting people into "rich" and "poor" thinkers is a big generalisation. Luck, family money and where you start matter too. The book also promotes the author's paid seminars. Keep the habits, skip the hype.

Napoleon Hill · 1937

Think and Grow Rich

6/10 Skim it

Read it for: Drive, persistence and having one clear goal. One of the best-selling success books ever written.

The main quotes, in plain English

  1. Top quote

    “Do not wait; the time will never be 'just right.'”

    p. 127

    What it means: There is never a perfect time to start. Waiting for one is how ideas die.

    Try this: Set a start date for your idea within the next 7 days, and tell someone.

  2. “All achievement, all earned riches, have their beginning in an idea!”

    p. 18

    What it means: Every business starts as an idea. But an idea is only the start: it needs a plan and customers.

    Try this: Run your idea through the Idea check to see how many customers it needs.

  3. “Temporary defeat should mean only one thing, the certain knowledge that there is something wrong with your plan.”

    p. 73

    What it means: A failure means "fix the plan", not "quit".

    Try this: After your next "no", write down one change you'll make to your plan.

  4. “There is no substitute for persistence”

    p. 86

    What it means: Most people quit too early. Steady effort beats talent that gives up.

    Try this: Commit to 30 outreach messages before you judge whether your idea works.

  5. “Success comes through the application of power, and power is attained through the cooperative efforts of other people.”

    p. 87

    What it means: You go further with others. Hill called a group that helps each other a "Master Mind".

    Try this: Find 2 people with goals of their own. Meet once a month to share progress.

⚠ Be careful: Written in 1937. Parts of it claim that thoughts send out "vibrations" and that wanting something badly enough brings money. There's no evidence for that. Historians have also questioned some stories Hill told about his own life. Keep the persistence, skip the magic.

David Cameron Gikandi · 2001

A Happy Pocket Full of Money

3/10 Skip, or skim

Read it for: Feeling calmer and more grateful about money. It's a mindset book, not a business-skills book.

The main quotes, in plain English

  1. Top quote

    “To build external wealth, build internal value and then exercise it.”

    What it means: Make yourself more valuable, with skills, reliability and effort, then use that value to help people. That's what customers pay for.

    Try this: Pick one skill that would make your business better and practise it for 30 days.

  2. “Not clearly knowing what you wish to have is a major cause of doubt and disbelief.”

    What it means: Vague goals make you unsure. Clear goals make the next step obvious.

    Try this: Write one money goal with an amount, a date and how you'll earn it.

  3. “Give first before you receive.”

    What it means: In business, giving value first, like a free sample or a helpful answer, builds the trust that leads to sales.

    Try this: Help 3 people in your target market this week without asking for anything.

⚠ Be careful: Much of the book says your thoughts directly create money, using ideas it links to quantum physics. That isn't how physics, or money, works. Money comes from creating value that people pay for.
