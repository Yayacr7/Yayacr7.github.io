Warning signs I found

    What to do now

    1. Don't pay, don't share codes and don't tap links until you're sure who it's from.
    2. Check it yourself. Search the name or the offer with the word "scam", and find the real company's contact details on your own, not from the message.
    3. Show a parent or guardian. A second look catches most scams, and you never have to deal with one alone.
    4. Ask someone you trust to look at it. A second look catches most scams.
    5. Report it and block it in the app or inbox it came from.
    6. Already paid or shared a code? Tell your bank or payment app now and change your passwords. Moving fast gives you the best chance of getting money back. In the US you can also report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

    The 6 money scams to know

    Scammers change the words, not the tricks.

    • Pay to get paid

      A job, "brand deal" or business that needs a fee, a starter kit or a course first.

      The tell: you pay before you earn.

    • Cash flip

      "Send me $50 and I'll turn it into $500 in an hour."

      The tell: money doesn't multiply in an hour. The $50 is gone.

    • Fake check

      A buyer or a new "boss" sends a check for too much, then asks you to send back the extra.

      The tell: the check bounces days later, and the money you sent is gone.

    • "Guaranteed" investing

      A "mentor" shows screenshots of huge crypto or trading profits.

      The tell: nobody can guarantee returns. Real investing goes up and down.

    • Code and log-in tricks

      Someone asks for the code that was just texted to you, or a message says your account is locked.

      The tell: that code opens your account. Never share it. Open the real app yourself.

    • Recruit your friends

      You earn by signing people up, not by selling to real customers.

      The tell: that's a pyramid scheme. Most people in one lose money.

    Cherry checks for common warning signs with fixed rules. She can't know who really sent a message, and scammers change their words, so "no big warning signs" never means "safe". When in doubt, don't pay. Under 18? Tell a parent or guardian before you send money or meet anyone.
